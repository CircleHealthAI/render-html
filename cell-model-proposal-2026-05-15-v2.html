Compliance QA · Cell Model Proposal (v2)

Working sketch · May 15, 2026 · grounded in Bridget's SUD RTC Audit (RenewalHG) · Track 1 reordered to retire her weekly Word doc fastest · updated May 19, 2026 with Track 2 (picklist parity)

Today's design feels wrong every time we look at it because it inherits from the LLM evaluation paradigm (one prompt, one response, one row) rather than the auditing paradigm (one ongoing question per patient with a current answer). Compliance is fundamentally an auditing product, not a chatbot product.

The unit of state should become a compliance cell: one cell per patient per criteria per compliance event (a deadline, a recurring period, or a discrete event like a new medication). AI runs become append-only history that writes into cells rather than creating new rows on each fire.

Three paths. Seven near-term fixes (§06 Track 1) retire Bridget's weekly Word doc on the current schema. Picklist parity (§06 Track 2, added 2026-05-19) layers a due-date picklist that mirrors today's trigger picklist onto a flatter criteria model, capturing most of the cell rebuild's structural value at roughly 10% of the engineering cost. The full cell-model rebuild (§06 Track 3) becomes the long-tail option for when scale, multi-phase cadences, or materialized per-period storage force it.

Decision (2026-05-19): Track 2 is the chosen structural path. Track 1 quality fixes ship in parallel. Track 3 stays documented as a long-tail option but is not on the roadmap.

01

What only the cell model unlocks

Once Track 1 ships, the rebuild's unique justification narrows to two structural items. Everything else Bridget and Nikali feel today (override loss, dashboard gaps, prompt mismatches, document-name fuzziness, the weekly Word doc itself) is fixable on the current schema.

  • Compliance expectation is structurally decoupled from how often AI checks. Today's trigger has to be both jobs at once: it decides when AI re-evaluates AND it implicitly defines what compliance expects. They are not the same thing, and conflating them is what produces the quietest bugs. A nightly trigger on a one-time question fires forever past the deadline. A weekly trigger that misses a fire silently equals a week of compliance unchecked. In the cell model, cadence on the criteria says what compliance expects; the sweep says when AI looks. Two layers, never coupled. You cannot retrofit this onto a model where the trigger does both jobs. See §02 for the config model.
  • Unified period-aware ledger for action items and trend reporting. Showing historical runs in the dashboard (Track 1 item #5) gets you per-patient visibility. What it does not give you is metric aggregation. "Org-wide week-over-week pass rate on Weekly Psych Notes" needs an expected denominator (number of weeks × patients in LOC), and the flat run history has no expected count, only observed runs. Per-clinician scorecards, cohort trend lines, and "X% of admissions had H&P on time this quarter" all live in the same period-aware ledger. Action items and trend reporting become two views over one data shape, not two separate pipelines.

Honesty update (2026-05-19): Track 2 (picklist parity) below closes most of both items without a cell rebuild. Adding a due-date picklist to each criteria that mirrors the trigger picklist gets you the decoupling in item 1 directly. Trend reporting in item 2 becomes derivable at query time by walking each patient's timeline against the due-date config and joining the existing run history. The remaining sliver that only the cell rebuild closes is materialized per-period storage: a performance and migration consideration at hundreds of facilities, not a capability one at today's scale.

02

Cadence and sweeps (the cell-model config)

Each criteria carries two separate configs. Both live in the criteria editor. Cadence creates cells. Sweep checks them.

Cadence is the compliance expectation: anchor + interval.

  • Anchor is the event that starts cell creation. Today's product allows three (admission, discharge, UR end), with two more in flight (facility admission, program start). The cell model adds event-typed anchors as a class: each new medication record, each status update, each clinical session note.
  • Interval is how cells materialize. Three patterns: once (one cell per anchor), every N days/weeks (one cell per period while a condition holds), per event (one cell per new instance).

Sweep declares when AI looks. Sweeps don't create cells, they update them. Two patterns can stack on one criteria:

  • Scheduled. Nightly, weekly, every N hours, on a specific day. Catches cells whose due dates moved without a clinical event firing.
  • Event-driven. On document upload, on note signature, on Kipu form save, on demand. Catches cells whose evidence just changed, so the dashboard reflects new clinical work in near-real-time.

A criteria can carry both. "Nightly + on note upload" means status refreshes throughout the day as work happens, with a daily catch-all so nothing rots.

Why two configs and not one: today's trigger does both jobs and that conflation is what produces the silent bugs (one-time questions refiring after the deadline, missed weekly periods going invisible, recurring schedules drifting with trigger frequency). Two layers, never coupled, makes those bugs impossible. The cost is real additional setup: per-criteria setup instead of assistant-level, and two configs per criteria instead of one. See §03 for the tradeoff.

03

What gets harder

  • Setup moves to the criteria level, and each criteria carries two configs instead of one. Today "an assistant" stands up 30 to 50 questions in a single config artifact (one trigger, one prompt set, one dashboard). The cell model forces per-criteria setup, with both a cadence and a sweep schedule, plus scope, status enum, and prompt. More clicks per setup, meaningfully more sweeps to configure. A shared criteria library helps customer 2+ pick rather than author, but customer 1 of any new audit is real work.
  • Dashboards become customizable, which means end-user setup becomes more important AND more work. Today the dashboard is whatever Circle hard-coded. Customizable filters and views mean users will not know what they're looking at without onboarding hand-holding. Expect materially more 1:1 setup time per customer and more "I don't see what I expected" support load.
  • Tags become necessary. To group "everything Bridget cares about Monday morning" or "everything Billing needs before claim submission," criteria need user-defined tags. Tags need maintenance, governance, and customer conventions.
04

How is this different from Kipu?

"Cells" sounds like an EMR concept, so the question is worth addressing. Kipu is the system of record for clinical facts; the cell model is the compliance interpretation over those facts. Different layer, not a replacement. Cells read evidence from Kipu, they do not store the underlying notes.

  • Kipu knows what happened. Cells know what was supposed to happen. If the clinician never wrote the H&P, Kipu has no row for it; the absence is invisible. A missing cell makes that absence first-class.
  • Kipu has no concept of compliance cadence. It records notes as written. It cannot say "this patient should have a weekly psych note for every 7 days in PHP." That's compliance policy, not EMR data.
  • Kipu's form metadata isn't an audit ledger. It doesn't carry late_signature vs unsigned vs pass, doesn't reconcile against the admission timestamp, doesn't run cross-document consistency, doesn't track human overrides across reruns.
  • Customer compliance policy varies; the EMR schema doesn't. Deadlines, required docs, and conditional rules differ by customer (and by state, payer, accreditation body). A criteria library normalizes the compliance side while reading evidence from each customer's specific Kipu schema.

06

Three-track path

Track 1 (in flight or planned, current V2 schema). Seven near-term fixes. Item #1 (Last Run flag) is deferred to Track 2 per the 2026-05-19 decision and shown below in muted form as a backup option. Item #5c (recurring vs one-time display) is similarly deferred since Track 2's due-date picklist provides this distinction natively. The remaining items ship in parallel with Track 2: items #2-#4 are the load-bearing tier; the bottom three close the gap to Bridget's 75-80% time-savings bar.

  1. deferred   Status-override persistence tied to a new per-trigger "Last Run" flag. Deferred to Track 2 (2026-05-19 decision). The three jobs this flag was doing decompose cleanly: cost control via stop-firing → due-date config (DEV-2130); override lock-in → per-period override behavior (DEV-2131); settled-vs-active distinction → the due_date_type field itself. Chris's episode work covers post-discharge fan-out. Revive only if Track 2 stalls. Original reasoning kept below.

    Triggers gain a Last Run setting: once an assistant has passed for a patient N times within the treatment episode, the trigger stops firing for that patient AND any manual status overrides on those questions lock in. On assistants without Last Run enabled (recurring weekly reviews), new runs overwrite as today so each week's actual status shows. Naively fixing the V2 override-persistence bug without this gate creates the opposite problem: a week-1 "pass" override sits on a weekly criteria forever and blocks future weeks from being evaluated. Triggers are assistant-level, which is fine for RenewalHG's structure because consent forms (Last Run on) and weekly progress notes (Last Run off) live in different assistants. Without this, Bridget has no memory of "items I've already confirmed in Kipu" across weeks, and the Word doc is the only place that memory lives.
  2. tier 1   Calendar awareness for review LLM evaluations. Inject current date, day-of-week, and explicit instructions for relative-time reasoning into the review LLM system prompt. Fixes a class of silent wrong answers happening right now: Nikali's weekend exclusion rule (AI can't tell a Saturday note from a Monday note), CodeMax + Bridget weekly recurring notes (AI hallucinates what "this past week" means and silently passes weeks the note wasn't written), "on Tuesday check Monday-Friday of last week" (no anchored notion of today). Sits above the Excel download because exporting wrong answers faster doesn't help Bridget; trustworthy evaluations on day 1 do. DEV-2136; note that the form-fill-scoped predecessor DEV-720 was canceled 5/17, this is the review LLM surface, different code path.
  3. tier 1   Downloadable Word/Excel report per facility per week. One document per facility, current clients, all failed items since intake, with the AI's justification text per failure. Format = Word (Bridget) or Excel filterable by assignee (Enea). Committed by Keshav on the 5/14 call. This is the literal replacement for Bridget's manual Word doc plus Smartsheet workflow.
  4. tier 1   Manual-status review criteria. A criteria type whose answer is human-entered, not AI-evaluated. Bridget audits a meaningful share of items today that Circle cannot answer from the Kipu API (facility compliance items, items in Google Drive, items requiring physical inspection). If the downloadable report covers only the AI-answerable items, Bridget has to manually add the rest to it every week, recreating the Word doc by hand. Manual-status criteria let her log those answers in Circle so the downloaded report covers her full audit, not a fractured half of it.
  5. tier 2   Expanded response status enum. Move from binary pass/fail to a richer set (missing_note, unsigned, late_signature, completed_not_timely, sent_to_clinician, N/A, manual_review). Bridget's Word doc carries this nuance by hand; the downloaded report needs to carry it natively. Three customers same day on 5/14 asked for the same expansion (RenewalHG + NJ Living Room + Consult Pros).
  6. tier 2   Dashboard improvements. (a) Admitted-vs-discharged filter so "never audited" patients surface. (b) Show historical runs alongside the latest run so Bridget can eyeball multi-week stragglers ("Alice's TX plan failed week 1, still failing week 3"); ISO-week bucketing on current schema, period bucketing swaps in when Track 2 lands. (c) Deferred to Track 2: recurring-vs-one-time display is provided natively by the due-date picklist (DEV-2130), so building a heuristic today (e.g., "does the trigger have intervalSeconds set?") is throwaway. (d) Facility-column filter that doesn't depend on LOC being set (engineering fix already in flight).
  7. tier 2   Document name and EMR field dictionary. Map EMR-specific document names to shared canonical terms once (for example "Treatment plan," "ASAM"). The same dictionary also covers key EMR field references: facility admission, level of care start date, utilization end date, discharge date, etc., mapped to the correct fields in each customer's EMR. Criteria prompts use the canonical names; no per-criteria fuzzy-match field. Same dictionary serves compliance review and form-fill. Quality fix that drives Bridget's false-positive needs-review rate down and approaches her 75-80% time-savings bar. Not strictly blocking for Word-doc retirement, but the most leveraged quality fix once the top three ship.

Track 2 (picklist parity, ~6 to 10 engineer-weeks). Added 2026-05-19. The insight: due date should be a peer field on each criteria using the same picklist of options the trigger already supports (72h after admission, weekly starting 7 days after admission, per new medication record, etc.). Trigger says when AI looks; due date says when compliance expects. Same option shapes, two independent fields. Once you make that move, most of what only the cell rebuild was supposed to unlock falls out at a fraction of the cost.

  1. tier 1   Criteria as the unit of state. Promote audit_question to a first-class criteria row that can live without a parent audit_template. Existing templates keep working as a saved view ("all criteria where template_id = X"); new criteria don't have to belong to one. This is the smallest change that unlocks everything below.
  2. tier 1   Due-date picklist on each criteria. Add a parallel block of fields that mirror the existing trigger schema (due_date_type, due_date_after_seconds, due_date_interval_seconds, due_date_schedule jsonb). Reuses every option the trigger already supports. The v1 trigger scheduler is unchanged. The dashboard reads the new fields to compute "what periods does this criteria cover for this patient, and which are currently due."
  3. tier 1   Tags. User-defined many-to-many on criteria. Replaces "assistant name" as the grouping primitive in dashboards. "Bridget's Monday Triage" becomes a saved filter on tag = bridget-priority, not a hard-coded template.
  4. tier 1   Per-period status override table. Small new table keyed by (patient_id, criteria_id, period_start). Where a manual "pass / N/A / late_signature" override lives without contaminating the runs table. Resolves the V2 override-loss bug structurally and supersedes the Last Run flag from Track 1 item #1.
  5. tier 2   Manual-status criteria. is_manual boolean on criteria. No AI run; status comes from human input. Absorbs Track 1 item #3, which becomes a one-field change once criteria exist as a first-class entity.
  6. tier 2   Bulk import. CSV-first (rows = criteria, columns = name, section, tags, due-date type, etc.) with a secondary markdown-paste path for agentic authoring. Customer with 150 criteria onboards in an afternoon, not a week.
  7. tier 2   Dashboard reads from criteria + due-date config + run history at query time. No materialized cell table. For each patient + criteria, walk the timeline using the due-date picklist to derive expected periods, join against the latest run in each period (or the override table), display. Trend reporting and per-clinician scorecards work as additional views over the same query shape.

What Track 2 unlocks. Decoupling of compliance expectation from AI check frequency (silent-bugs class from §01 item 1). Expected denominator for trend reporting, derived at query time (§01 item 2, without a physical cell table). Override persistence per period, not per run. Same flat-criteria + tags + saved-views decomposition the Track 3 rebuild ends at.

What Track 2 deliberately leaves to Track 3. Multi-phase criteria like the Outcome Measure (72h + every 14d + pre-DC) don't fit one due-date entry; workaround is to split into three sibling criteria tagged outcome-measure and group by tag, which is acceptable for v1. No materialized cell table, so reporting recomputes period membership at query time (fine at today's scale, starts to hurt at hundreds of facilities). Append-only per-cell history is approximated by querying the existing runs table by period, which is workable but less queryable than a real cells ledger.

Track 3 (cell-model rebuild, ~6 to 12 engineer-months). Deferred per the 2026-05-19 decision. Only justified if Track 2 hits its limits: multi-phase criteria become a top complaint, scale forces materialized per-period storage, or a billing product needs a real append-only ledger. Rebuild around the cell as the unit of state for the two things Track 1 fundamentally cannot do (and Track 2 only approximates):

  1. Structurally decouple compliance expectation from runtime check via two physical layers (cadence creates cells, sweep updates them) rather than two parallel picklists on one row. The class of silent bugs caused by the conflated trigger stops being possible by construction, not by convention.
  2. Materialized period-aware ledger for action items and trend reporting. One physical table with an expected denominator powers per-patient action queues, org-wide trend lines, per-clinician scorecards, and cohort comparisons without recomputing period membership on every query. Append-only history per cell becomes a first-class data shape, not a derived view.

Compliance also gets a true criteria library, assistants become saved views, triggers become a runtime concept rather than a config concept, and the foundation is ready for billing products without a second rebuild.

Engineering scope, Track 2 vs Track 3. Track 2 is mostly mechanical because the trigger picklist it mirrors already exists in v2 (chart_review_template_triggers with triggerType, timeAfterTriggerSeconds, intervalSeconds, schedule jsonb). The v1 scheduler runtime (lib/integrations/chart-review/ready-triggers.ts) needs no change. New code is roughly: one criteria-table relationship change, one due-date-fields block, one overrides table, one tags M2M, a CSV importer, and a dashboard query layer that joins criteria → runs → overrides through a derived-periods CTE. Track 3 requires a new cells table, a cell-generator background job firing on both clinical events and scheduled sweeps, a rewrite of the dashboard query layer to read from cells, a migration plan for existing run data, retirement of the trigger table (since "trigger" becomes a runtime concept), and per-customer reinterpretation of every existing review template setup. The gap is roughly 10x in engineering cost for the last 10 to 15% of the unlock at today's scale.

Combined priority across Track 1 and Track 2

Ordering ignores engineering effort. Pure value and leverage. Track 1 items ship in days to weeks; Track 2 items ship in weeks to months. The bundle at #4 must ship together: criteria as the unit of state is unusable in practice without tags (for grouping in the dashboard) and bulk import (for onboarding any customer with more than ~20 criteria). DEV-1937 is ranked above DEV-2130 and DEV-2131 because the dictionary benefits every customer on every run from day one, while the Track 2 architectural changes only deliver value once customers adopt the new criteria model. Reasonable to flip DEV-1937 and DEV-2131 if you weight "Bridget's overrides are getting wiped right now" higher than "every chart review run gets more accurate."

08

Grounding in Bridget's audit

Sanity-checked against Bridget's SUD RTC Audit at RenewalHG: 74 distinct criteria across 17 sections (Facesheet, Pre-Admission, Medical Intake, Clinical Assessments, Treatment Planning, Progress Notes, Discharge, etc.). The cell model handles all 74. Most map directly. The rest fit a small set of recurring patterns: dynamic form lists, chained conditionals (SI risk triggers Safety Plan + SI Reassessment), retrospective windows (OM within 3 days of DC), per-event criteria (psychotropic auth per new med, signing per status update), inverted predicates (family therapy unless declination), and detox-end gates on recurring weeklies.

Cell volume scales with length of stay. A 5-day AMA generates ~80 to 100 cells (some N/A from early discharge). A 32-day RTC completion generates ~300 to 450 (more if PHP step-down pulls in daily group counts). Dashboards aggregate by default. Cell count shows up as queryability, not as complexity for the user, assuming the view is set up right.
